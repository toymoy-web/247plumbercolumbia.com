If you smell gas: do this first

Smelling gas is a safety event first and a repair question second.

  • 1Leave the building. Take everyone and the pets. Do not switch on lights or use a phone inside.
  • 2Call 911 from outside. Fire crews are trained for gas leaks.
  • 3Call your gas provider. Dominion Energy South Carolina takes gas leak calls at 800-815-0083. If another provider serves you, use the number on your bill.
  • 4Do not return until cleared. Wait for crews to say it is safe.

How a gas line is installed

Pipe size comes from the total load of all appliances and the length of the run. Black iron, flexible stainless tubing and approved flex connectors each have rules. Corrugated stainless tubing must be bonded to the building's electrical grounding system under the National Electrical Code (NEC), so this is a job where plumbing and electrical rules meet.

South Carolina adopts the International Fuel Gas Code (IFGC) with state modifications. Before the line is concealed or put in service, it is pressure tested. A soap solution or electronic leak detector checks every joint.

Who owns what, and why it matters

The gas utility typically owns the line up to and including the meter. The homeowner owns the piping beyond it. The utility does not repair house piping, and a plumber does not touch the meter. That boundary decides who to call.

Permits and inspections are common for new gas lines. Your building department sets the rules, and an inspection protects you at resale.

Newer homes versus older ones

Newer homes in places like Lexington have gas stubs in the walls for common appliances, so most work is connection and testing. Older homes in West Columbia or Cayce may have aging black iron that corrodes at joints, and an inspection before adding a load is wise.

Emergency gas leak: leave first, call the gas utility, then call a plumber

If you smell gas, hear hissing or see a damaged line, leave the house and call 911 and your gas utility from outside. Do not flip switches, use a phone inside or hunt for the leak yourself. Dominion Energy South Carolina lists 800-815-0083 for gas leaks.

The utility makes the area safe and may shut off the meter. After that we handle the plumbing side: finding the leak in the house piping, repairing or replacing the failed section and pressure testing before gas is restored.

In most cases the utility restores service at the meter once the repair passes. A plumber does not reopen the meter on its own.

Gas leak checks and clearance reports

Not every gas call is an emergency. Buyers, landlords and homeowners ask for an inspection to confirm there is no leak, to check a furnace and water heater after a smell or to get a written report for an insurer or a closing.

We pressure test the house piping, check each appliance connection with a leak solution and note the results in writing. A report that says no leak found describes the day of the test only, and it is not a utility or code inspection.

Gas fireplaces, stoves and propane conversions are separate jobs that start with the same pressure test. Ask about them when you call.

Shutoffs, flex connectors and carbon monoxide

Every gas appliance should have its own shutoff valve within reach. Old connectors can corrode or crack, and a newer corrugated stainless connector should replace them. A carbon monoxide detector on each level is a separate safety layer, because a gas appliance that vents poorly makes carbon monoxide without any smell of gas.

Pressure testing and what the inspector looks for

Before a new line is covered or put in service, it is pressure tested with air or an inert gas and the gauge is watched for a drop. The National Fuel Gas Code (NFPA 54) and the International Fuel Gas Code cover the same ground, and South Carolina adopts the latter. Inspectors look at pipe size, supports, shutoff placement, sediment traps and the way flexible connectors are used. Keep the permit paperwork with your house records.

If you smell gas after the work is done, the same safety steps apply. Leave, call 911 and call your gas provider. Do not try to find the leak yourself.

Gas permits, the utility and the county

Dominion Energy South Carolina owns the line to the meter and takes leak calls at 800-815-0083. Everything beyond the meter is yours, and new lines usually need a permit from the city or county building office in Richland or Lexington County. Outdoor grill and generator hookups follow the same permit path, whether the patio is near the Congaree Riverwalk or the deck is on Lake Murray.

Gas lines in older houses and around the lake

Dominion Energy South Carolina serves gas to many Midlands homes, but some houses around Lake Murray and Chapin use propane tanks, which changes the regulator and the piping. Tell us which fuel you have before we quote.

In older Shandon and Elmwood Park houses, a gas range or heater may still run on aging black iron with dated shutoffs. A pressure test and new shutoff valves are the common upgrades.
