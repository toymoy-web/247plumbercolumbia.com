A growing town on the Richland and Fairfield line

Blythewood sits in both Richland and Fairfield counties, and its population grew from 2,034 in 2010 to 4,772 in 2020. Town hall is the George P. Hoffman House, built in 1855, but most of the housing around it is far newer. A large share of our calls here come from subdivisions built in the past two decades.

Newer does not mean trouble free. Builder-grade tank heaters often start to fail somewhere between years 8 and 12, and shortcuts that were hidden at closing show up later as slow drains or uneven pressure. A look at the water heater or a camera run through the drain line usually settles it. Emergency dispatch runs around the clock, so a failed water heater in a new subdivision at 2 a.m. gets the same response as a call at noon.

Water from Winnsboro and what it means for your tap

The Town of Blythewood buys its water from the Town of Winnsboro, an arrangement that began around 2000 and has been renewed for another 20 years. That means quality reports for Columbia Water do not describe what comes out of a Blythewood faucet. Addresses outside the town limits may be served by another utility or by a private well.

Before you buy a softener or a filter, ask your supplier for its latest water quality report. Compare the numbers with what you actually see at the fixtures, such as spotting on glass or a taste you dislike. A test result beats a guess every time.

Before we arrive: a burst pipe or active leak in Blythewood

Newer houses here often keep the main shutoff in a garage or utility closet. Work through these four steps while help is on the way.

1

Shut off the main valve

Check the garage, the utility closet and the area around the water heater for the valve where the line enters the house. A quarter-turn lever closes at once and a round handle needs several turns. If it will not close, say so on the call, because the shutoff at the meter belongs to your utility.

2

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near outlets, a ceiling light or the panel, switch off those breakers only when the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. A wet panel means stay clear and tell us. Dry your hands before you touch any switch.

3

Clear the area

Move rugs, electronics and stored boxes out of the path of the water. Photograph the damage before you clean up, because insurers and builders both ask for proof. Keep children and pets out of any wet room.

Four problems we see in newer Blythewood homes

These turn up most in houses built since the early 2000s.

A water heater near the end

Rumbling, rusty hot water or a damp pan under the tank means it is time to talk about replacement instead of another repair.

Slow drains in a newer bathroom

Builder debris and low-slope runs catch hair and paste. Drain cleaning clears the clog, and a camera shows whether anything worse is behind it.

Pressure that drops with two fixtures running

A failing pressure regulator or a half-closed valve is the usual cause. We check both before suggesting anything bigger.

A soft patch in the yard

On an acreage lot, a wet spot between the meter and the house can mean a leaking water service line.

Acreage lots, septic tanks and wells at the edge of town

Outside the town's water lines, many Blythewood-area homes sit on larger lots. Some use septic tanks or private wells, and the repair path differs from a house on town service. Tell us at the start of a call which system you have.

Septic pumping and well drilling are separate trades. What a plumber handles is the plumbing on the house side of them, such as a backed-up house drain, a pressure problem or a filtration setup for well water.

Commercial calls along Wilson Boulevard

The US 21 corridor, called Wilson Boulevard through town, carries shops, offices and light industrial buildings. Blythewood is also home to the US headquarters of Spirax-Sarco Engineering, a sign of how much non-residential building the area attracts.

Restaurants, clinics and warehouses need floor drains, grease control and backflow testing on irrigation. Our commercial plumbing page lists what we handle, and emergencies use the same 24/7 line. Irrigation assemblies follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as South Carolina adopts it.
