Sewer Smell in Your Columbia, SC House: Six Causes and Fixes

Rotten-egg odors escape through dry traps, blocked vents, tired wax rings and cracked drains. Six checks sort the cause, mostly with a glass of water and a flashlight.

Written by the Columbia 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 3, 2026.

What the smell actually is

Sewer gas is a mix of gases produced as waste breaks down. Hydrogen sulfide gives it the rotten-egg odor, and methane is odorless but flammable. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires every fixture to have a trap and a vent for exactly this reason: the water-filled trap and the vent system keep this gas out of the house.

A smell means that barrier has failed somewhere. Low levels are mainly a nuisance, but high levels can be hazardous, so a strong or persistent odor deserves attention. If you feel ill or smell gas strongly, leave and call for help.

Test the easy ones first

These steps take about ten minutes.

  • 1Run water in every drain. Include the guest bath, the laundry and any floor drain. Let each run for a minute.
  • 2Smell each fixture. A smell that follows one fixture points to that trap or seal.
  • 3Check the toilet bases. Look for dampness or a rocking bowl.
  • 4Look at outdoor and crawlspace caps. A missing cap lets gas escape.
  • 5Check the roof vents. Ask someone to look from a ladder, or use binoculars.

Why the smell comes back

A trap that dries out once will dry out again. In a seldom-used bathroom, a little water poured in monthly keeps the seal, or a few drops of mineral oil on top slow evaporation. Floor drains in utility rooms are the most common repeat offenders.

If the smell returns after you refill the trap, the cause is elsewhere, and a plumber should check the vent and the drain line. Our page on drain cleaning explains how a camera finds blockages and buildup.

When the smell points to the sewer line

A smell in the yard or crawlspace that gets stronger after rain, damp patches over the line or a basement smell that spreads suggest a leak in the main. Roots, cracked clay or broken cast iron are the usual causes. A camera inspection finds the damage. Our page on sewer line repair covers what a repair involves.

Older sections of West Columbia, where mill-era houses sit on aging lines, are one example of where this shows up, and you can read more at West Columbia.

Smells from the disposal and the dishwasher

A garbage disposal holds food waste, and a splash guard collects grime. Grind ice and a lemon peel, and scrub under the rubber flaps. A dishwasher can smell like sewage if its drain loop is wrong or its filter is dirty. Clean the filter and run a hot cycle.

If the smell stays after cleaning, the problem may be in the kitchen drain line, which a plumber can inspect with a camera. See garbage disposal repair for the disposal side.

Staying safe around sewer gas

Hydrogen sulfide is toxic at high concentrations, and methane is flammable. In a house, the levels are usually low, but do not tolerate a strong or lasting smell. Ventilate, avoid open flames and call a plumber. If you smell natural gas, which has an added rotten-egg odor, leave and call 911 and your gas provider. Dominion Energy South Carolina takes leak reports at 800-815-0083.

A sewer smell with symptoms like headache or nausea calls for fresh air and medical advice, not a wait-and-see approach.

Toilets, wax rings and sealing the floor

A toilet that smells at the base may have a failed wax ring. Water or gas escapes at the seal, and the smell often comes with a damp floor. Do not ignore it, since a leak can rot the subfloor. Replacing the ring and checking the flange is a short job. See toilet repair for how it is done.
