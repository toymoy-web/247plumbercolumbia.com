Emergency Faucet, Valve & Sink Repair in Columbia, SC

A burst supply valve, a spray under the sink or a faucet that will not shut off is a same-hour problem. Most drips come from a worn cartridge or washer, and most puddles under a sink come from a loose nut or a tired supply hose.

Leaks under the sink: find the source first

Dry the area, put a paper towel on the cabinet floor, and check these in order.

  • 1The supply lines. Braided hoses and their nuts can loosen or burst.
  • 2The shutoff valves. Old valves drip at the stem and often fail when turned.
  • 3The P-trap. Slip nuts loosen and seals crack.
  • 4The sink drain and basket. Putty and gaskets dry out.
  • 5The faucet base. Water running down the underside points to a failed cartridge or O-ring.

Valve burst or leak under the sink?

Close the main valve or the small valves under the sink and call any hour.

Low flow at the tap

The aerator at the tip of the spout catches debris. After plumbing work or a main disturbance, sediment can clog it. Unscrew it, rinse it and reinstall it before assuming a bigger problem.

If every faucet has weak flow, the cause is elsewhere. A failing pressure regulator or a corroded supply line is more likely, and repiping or water line repair may be the real answer.

Old shutoffs and old pipe in older houses

Homes in Forest Acres and the older blocks of West Columbia often have original shutoff valves and galvanized stubs. A valve that has not moved in thirty years can fail when turned. A good technician replaces it during the faucet job, so you are not left with a leak at the wall.

Replacing a faucet: what to know

Measure the sink holes, one, two or three, before shopping. A faucet that uses fewer holes than the sink needs a deck plate. Choose solid-brass bodies where you can, and avoid plastic internals.

Columbia Water is soft, so mineral buildup on spouts is mild for city customers. Rubber parts are the ones to watch, which is why chloramine-resistant seals are worth asking for.

Emergency valve and supply line failures: water you cannot shut off

A burst supply valve is one of the most urgent small repairs. Water sprays from behind a toilet, from a wall near a shower or from a line behind a washing machine, and the usual shutoff is the part that failed. The fix starts at the main.

Close the main valve at the house. If the main will not close, say so on the call, because that is a second repair and we bring parts for it. Turn off breakers for a wet room only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.

We replace the failed valve, supply line or fitting, and we recommend swapping old multi-turn valves for quarter-turn valves so the next shutoff works the first time.

Shower valves, tub faucets and outdoor spigots

A shower valve that drips after shutoff, a tub faucet that will not stop and an outdoor spigot that leaks inside the wall are common repair calls. Most come down to a worn cartridge, stem or seat, but a spigot that leaks behind the wall can damage framing and should be checked quickly.

Some callers also ask for new outdoor faucets or winterizing, which are quick jobs when the supply line is accessible.

Choosing a replacement faucet

Federal rules cap most faucets at 2.2 gallons per minute, and WaterSense bathroom faucets use 1.5. A lower flow saves water and usually does not feel weaker. Look for a solid brass body and a ceramic-disc or cartridge valve, which hold up better than plastic parts. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for shutoff valves on most fixture supplies, so ask for new stops if yours are old.

Check the finish too. Brushed and matte finishes hide spots, while polished chrome shows every mark. Match the finish across the room if you can.

Hot side or cold side: reading the drip

A drip from the spout that stops when you close the hot valve points to the hot-side seat or cartridge. A leak at the handle usually means a worn O-ring or packing. A faucet that drips only when the handle is in one position is often a worn cartridge. Describing the pattern when you call helps a technician bring the right part.

Kitchen faucet features that are worth paying for

A pull-down sprayer saves more time than any other feature in a kitchen, because it rinses a sink corner or fills a tall pot without moving it. A spout that swings reaches both bowls of a double sink. Touch controls sound modern, but they rely on batteries and sensors that fail before the valve does.

Skip designs with long, thin cartridges that are hard to find. Before you buy, check that the maker still sells the cartridge, because a faucet you cannot repair becomes a faucet you replace.

Old valves in older Columbia neighborhoods

Older homes in Forest Acres and the Five Points area of Richland County can still have original shutoff valves, and a seized valve is what turns a quick cartridge swap into a longer job. Columbia Water is soft, so mineral buildup is mild, and Lexington County wells are the exception worth testing.

Faucet and sink calls from Elmwood Park to Lake Carolina

Pedestal sinks, cast iron tubs and old compression faucets are part of the charm in Elmwood Park's early 1900s houses, and they have their own repair parts. A drip there may need a stem and seat set instead of a cartridge.

Newer homes around Lake Carolina and Blythewood usually have single-handle cartridge faucets, where a cartridge or a supply line is the usual repair.
