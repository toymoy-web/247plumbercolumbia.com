After-hours priorities

When something fails at night, these steps limit the damage.

  • 1Shut off the affected water. Know the zone valves and the main for the building.
  • 2Keep the public away. Cone off wet floors and close restrooms.
  • 3Photograph the loss. Insurers and landlords both ask for it.
  • 4Call the plumber and the landlord or manager. Leases often set who authorizes repairs.

Grease, floor drains and the utility's rules

Food businesses must manage fats, oils and grease. A grease interceptor that is not pumped on schedule sends solids into the sewer, and utilities can penalize the business. Jetting the line without servicing the interceptor only moves the problem downstream. Ask your utility about its rules, and see our hydro jetting page for line cleaning. Interceptors and floor drain traps are also covered by the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as South Carolina adopts it.

Older buildings in older districts

Parts of Columbia, such as the Five Points area and the Congaree Vista, have older commercial buildings with their own piping history. Cast iron stacks, hidden branch lines and shared walls make a camera look worthwhile before large repairs.

In West Columbia, the New Brookland blocks have mill-era buildings with similar questions. West Columbia explains its housing history, which also applies to its older storefronts.

Property managers and work orders

Managers need clear reports. Ask for the problem description, the cause, the repair and photos, so the invoice matches the work order. For multi-unit buildings, a camera inspection of the shared lines identifies which tenant's problem is actually a building problem.

Commercial properties with irrigation or fire lines often have backflow assemblies due for annual tests, covered on our backflow testing page.

Emergency commercial plumbing: floor drains, grates and after-hours leaks

Business emergencies rarely wait for opening hours. An overflowing floor drain, a steel drain cover that has to come off immediately, standing water in an electrical room or an animal trapped in a drain line all need quick action and a clear point of contact.

Tell us the building type, the suite or bay and who can give access. Property managers often send work orders, so we confirm the address, the contact and the scope in writing so the right person is on site.

Keep customers and staff away from standing water and keep electrical equipment off it. We stop the flow, clear the drain and write down what we found for the manager.

Leak detection for shops, plazas and apartment complexes

Commercial leak calls include a leak in a business property, water coming up in an apartment complex parking lot, a utility notice that a property keeps losing water and a request for a leak survey of a larger site such as a resort.

We start with the meters and isolation valves to see which building or branch is losing water, then use acoustic tools on the suspect section. Multi-unit properties benefit from closing building valves one at a time while the meter is watched.

A spot test on one suspected line costs less than a survey of a whole site, so tell us which you want. We write up the result so a manager can act on it.

What to do between emergencies: a maintenance checklist for businesses

Most commercial emergencies were visible weeks before.

  • 1Floor drains and traps. Flush dry drains regularly so sewer gas does not enter.
  • 2Grease interceptors. Pump on a schedule set by use, not on failure.
  • 3Water heaters. Flush yearly and check the relief valve.
  • 4Restroom fixtures. Replace worn flush valves and supply stops before they leak.
  • 5Backflow assemblies. Test annually and file the report.

Flushometers, water heaters and recovery rate

Commercial restrooms often use flushometer valves, which need regular diaphragm replacement. Commercial water heaters are sized by recovery rate, how fast they reheat, not only by tank size. A restaurant or a gym needs far more hot water in a short window than an office does.

If the hot water runs out at rush hour, the unit may be undersized or sediment may have cut its output. Either problem can be checked on a service call.

Documentation commercial customers should expect

A good service invoice states the location, the cause, the repair, the parts and the time. Landlords, tenants and insurers all ask for it later. Photos before and after, and notes on anything that needs follow-up, keep arguments about responsibility short.

Commercial districts and their utilities

Older commercial buildings around Five Points and the Congaree Vista in Richland County have their own pipe histories, and restaurants there answer to Columbia Water on grease and to the South Carolina Department of Environmental Services on wastewater. Businesses along Sunset Boulevard in Lexington County deal with other utilities, so confirm yours.

Event-day demand near Williams-Brice Stadium and the Fairgrounds

Williams-Brice Stadium opened in 1934 beside the South Carolina State Fairgrounds, at George Rogers Boulevard and Bluff Road. Restaurants, hotels and shops around that corner see heavy water use on event days, and drains, grease lines and restrooms are what fail first.

Blythewood's Wilson Boulevard corridor and the Two Notch Road corridor in Northeast Columbia show the same pattern in smaller doses. A commercial emergency uses the same 24/7 line, and we ask about the building's age, its tenants and where the shutoff sits.
