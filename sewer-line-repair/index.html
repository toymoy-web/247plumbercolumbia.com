Signs the problem is the sewer line

A single slow drain is local. These patterns point to the line that leaves the house.

Several drains back up together

Fixtures share the main. When the lowest ones bubble or fill first, the main is blocked.

A sewage smell in the yard

A cracked line leaks into the soil and the smell travels up.

Lush green or sunken patches over the line

Leaking effluent feeds grass, and washed-out soil leaves a dip.

Drains clear and then return

Roots or a belly in the pipe collect solids again.

Camera first: how the break is found

A small camera on a flexible cable runs from the cleanout through the line and records every joint. Distance counters and a locator on the surface mark the exact spot of the damage, so a trench opens in one place and not along the whole yard.

Ask for the recording. It lets you compare quotes and, after repair, shows the line is clean. It also helps when you sell the house. Some contractors code defects using the NASSCO Pipeline Assessment Certification Program (PACP) system, which makes recordings easier to compare.

Why older Columbia-area lines fail

Clay and cast iron sewer lines laid decades ago crack at joints, and roots exploit every gap. Cayce saw major postwar growth after the Blossom Street Bridge opened in the early 1950s. Houses from that period are now seventy-plus years old.

In the flood-prone parts of the Congaree and Gills Creek watersheds, saturated ground also pushes groundwater into cracked lines, which overloads the public sewer during storms.

Who is responsible for which part

The lateral that runs from your house to the public sewer is usually the homeowner's responsibility, up to the connection at the main. The main belongs to the utility. Responsibility lines vary by utility, so confirm yours before you pay for work in the street. Repairs must meet the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as South Carolina adopts it, and work in the right of way may be inspected.

Call 811 before any excavation. The free locate marks buried utilities and prevents a gas or water line strike.

What to do before the camera arrives

A little preparation lets the first visit answer more questions.

  • 1Stop using water. Every flush adds to what is already backing up.
  • 2Locate the cleanout. It is usually a capped pipe near the foundation or in the yard.
  • 3Note the symptoms and timing. A list of which fixtures back up and when helps the diagnosis.
  • 4Protect floors. Sewage that has already come up is a contamination risk, so keep people out of the area.

Backwater valves for low-lying lots

A backwater valve is a check valve on the building drain that lets sewage flow out but not back in. It is a sensible add-on for a lot that sits below the manhole upstream, and it needs a cleanout nearby and an annual check, because debris can hold the flap open.

Emergency sewer backup response: what happens in the first hour

A sewer backup is an emergency because the water carries waste. Signs include sewage in a shower or tub, bubbling toilets, water rising through a floor drain and several fixtures draining slowly at once. Stop using water and keep people and pets out of the affected room.

On arrival we find the cleanout, clear the blockage enough to stop the backup and run a camera to see why it happened. A one-off clog needs only cleaning. A broken, sagging or root-filled pipe needs repair, and the camera footage becomes the record for the repair quote and for insurance.

Ask for the footage and a note of the distance from the cleanout to the problem, since that number tells a dig crew where to start.

Septic line problems on properties outside the sewer system

Not every property near Columbia connects to city sewer. Callers with septic systems describe water coming up under a tub, a drain that backs up when the whole family is home, a cracked joint, a sagging pipe between the house and the tank and a line that needs lining.

The plumbing from the house to the tank is plumbing work. The tank and drain field belong to a septic contractor, and in South Carolina septic permits come through the state environmental agency. We tell you which side the problem is on before anyone digs.

A camera run from the cleanout shows whether the pipe has sagged, cracked or filled with roots. A sagging section holds water and solids, and the fix is relaying that section at the right slope.

Who to call when the break is in the street

Inside the city of Columbia, the owner maintains the sewer line from the house to the property line, and Columbia Water owns it from the property line to the main. Columbia Water handles those mains, Cayce and West Columbia handle their own, and the South Carolina Department of Environmental Services regulates wastewater across Richland County and Lexington County. Roots, clay and age are the usual causes of a failed lateral anywhere in the Midlands.

What a camera inspection shows

A camera on a flexible cable travels the line and records what it finds. The usual findings are roots at the joints, cracked or offset pipe, a sag where water collects, and scale on old cast iron. A locator tracks the camera from above so the depth and position of the problem are marked in the yard.

That recording is the basis for the repair and for a fair quote. It also helps a buyer before closing, since a failing lateral is expensive to inherit.

Spot dig, lining or replacement

When one section has failed, a spot repair digs to that section and replaces it with new pipe. Trenchless lining can restore a line that has cracks but still holds its shape, because a resin liner is cured inside the old pipe. A line that has collapsed or sagged badly is a candidate for sewer line replacement.

Clearing roots first with hydro jetting often shows how much pipe is left. Mid-century lines may be clay, cast iron or fiber pipe such as Orangeburg, which can deform over time, so the camera result decides which repair fits.

Before the dig: locates, permits and the cleanout

Call 811 before any digging so buried utilities are marked. Work that reaches the right of way may need approval from the city or county, and inside the city of Columbia the owner maintains the line from the house to the property line.

A cleanout cap in the yard gives access for the camera and for clearing a blockage without opening the line. If you cannot find one, say so when you call, and the technician can tell you where one should go.

Signs the problem is the lateral and not one drain

One slow sink is almost always a branch clog. When several fixtures drain slowly together, when the lowest drain in the house backs up first, or when a toilet gurgles as the washer runs, the main line is the likely culprit.

Outside, a soggy patch of lawn, a sewage smell in the yard or a sunken strip over the line can mean the pipe has broken. A floor drain that backs up after heavy rain can also point to a failing line. Say which of these you see when you call, so dispatch plans for a camera on the first visit.

How to protect the line afterward

Keep grease, wipes and paper towels out of the drains, and avoid planting trees with aggressive roots over the lateral. A yearly camera check or a preventive cleaning is worth it on an older line that has already had roots.

Keep the camera recording with your records, since it shows the condition of the pipe on the day you bought or repaired it.

Repairs under the mature trees around Earlewood Park

Mature trees are the main enemy of old sewer laterals. Around Earlewood Park, a 66-acre urban forest given to the city in 1925, and along the shaded streets of Shandon and Forest Acres, roots find the joints in clay pipe and grow into them.

A camera shows where the roots entered and whether a spot repair or a liner will hold. If the pipe has collapsed or sagged, replacement becomes the honest answer.
