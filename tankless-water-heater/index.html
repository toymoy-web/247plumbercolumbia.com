Cold water in the shower, codes on the display: what is going on?

Homeowners rarely search for a part name. They describe what the shower does. Here is what the common complaints usually mean.

Hot, then cold, then hot

Often a flow-rate or sensor issue. The unit needs a minimum flow to fire, and a clogged inlet screen can drop flow below it.

Error code on the display

Codes point to ignition, venting, flame or temperature sensors. The manual names the code, and the cause still needs testing.

Water is lukewarm at full flow

The unit may be undersized for the demand, or scale or a gas supply shortfall is limiting output.

A rotten-egg smell at the unit

Treat it as a gas leak. Leave, call 911, and call Dominion Energy South Carolina at 800-815-0083 or your own gas provider.

How a flush works

A flush circulates a descaling solution or plain water through the heat exchanger using a small pump and hoses on the service valves. The inlet filter screen is cleaned at the same time, which fixes many low-flow complaints.

Units with condensate drains also need the neutralizer checked. A blocked condensate line can shut a high-efficiency unit down, and it is a simple fix once someone looks.

Gas line and venting: the usual hidden problem

A tankless unit can draw a large burst of gas. Older homes in Forest Acres or West Columbia may have a gas line sized for a tank heater, and the new unit starves. The symptom is a unit that fires, then drops out under load.

A proper install checks the gas line size against the unit's rated input, follows the International Fuel Gas Code (IFGC) as South Carolina adopts it, and uses venting rated for the model. See gas line installation and repair when the supply is the bottleneck.

Sizing: flow rate and temperature rise

A tankless heater is rated by how many gallons per minute it can heat by a given temperature rise. Winter incoming water is colder than summer water, so the same unit that keeps up in July can fall short in January. Add up the fixtures likely to run at once: a shower at about 2 gallons per minute, a dishwasher and a kitchen tap.

An undersized unit gives lukewarm water when two fixtures run together. A flow restrictor or a second unit is a fix. Replacement with a larger model is another. Relief valve piping and condensate drains follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as South Carolina adopts it.

A simple maintenance calendar

Following this schedule protects the warranty and the heat exchanger.

  • 1Every year. Flush the heat exchanger and clean the inlet screen, following your manual.
  • 2After any plumbing work. Check the inlet screen again for debris.
  • 3Every year, condensing units. Check the condensate drain and neutralizer.
  • 4Every few years. Have a technician inspect the vent, burner and combustion.

What to do if your tankless heater shuts down or throws a code: safety steps

Repeated resets can hide a real fault. Work through these before you try again.

  • 1Do not reset it over and over. Repeated ignition attempts can flood a vent or stress parts. Reset once, then stop.
  • 2Check for a gas smell or soot. Leave and call 911 and your gas provider if you smell gas. Soot at the unit points to a venting fault.
  • 3Look at the vent and the condensate line. A blocked or disconnected vent should never be left running.
  • 4Write down the code and call. The code, the brand and the age of the unit tell us what to bring.

Gas load, the utility and the county

A tankless upgrade can raise the gas your house draws at one moment. Dominion Energy South Carolina is the gas utility for many Midlands homes, so ask whether a larger meter or regulator is needed. Houses in Lexington County that were built for a tank heater near Lake Murray and older Richland County homes both turn up this issue.

Flow numbers that decide the model

A shower uses about 2 gallons per minute and a kitchen tap about 1.5, so two showers and a dishwasher can call for 5 gallons per minute or more. Incoming water in winter is colder than in summer, which raises the temperature rise the unit must deliver and shrinks its output.

Where tankless fits in different Columbia housing

Tankless units need gas capacity, venting and wall space. Newer homes in Northeast Columbia, Lake Carolina and Blythewood usually have room to mount a unit and run a vent, while early 1900s bungalows in Shandon and Earlewood may need a vent route and a gas line upgrade first.

Ask for a gas load calculation before any quote. It shows whether your meter and piping can feed a unit that fires at full rate while two showers run.

When replacement beats another repair

Tankless units commonly last far longer than tanks when maintained, so a young unit with a failed part is a repair. A unit with a cracked heat exchanger is a replacement.
