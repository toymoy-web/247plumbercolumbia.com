The first ten minutes of a burst pipe emergency

Speed matters more than perfection. Work through these in order.

  • 1Close the main valve. Turn it clockwise. If you cannot find it, close the valve at the meter.
  • 2Open a low faucet. It drains the line and drops pressure so less water escapes.
  • 3Cut power to wet areas. If water reaches outlets or the panel, shut the breaker only if you can do it from dry ground. Otherwise leave and call.
  • 4Move what you can. Lift rugs, electronics and paper off the floor.
  • 5Photograph and call. Take photos before cleanup, then call us and your insurer.

What a burst pipe repair involves

The technician locates the failure, cuts out the damaged section and joins new pipe with the right fittings. Mixed materials need proper transition fittings, because joining copper to galvanized without them creates a corrosion problem.

If the pipe that burst is the oldest section of a worn line, expect the technician to suggest repiping. A patch on a failing run is often followed by another failure nearby.

Repair is one job, cleanup is another

We repair the pipe. If water has soaked drywall, insulation or flooring, the drying and cleanup are a separate service with a separate phone line. Our water damage restoration page explains what that involves, and wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold, according to EPA guidance.

Keep the two jobs distinct in your insurance claim too. The plumber's invoice documents the failure, and the restoration estimate documents the damage.

Frozen pipes and cold snaps

A pipe that froze may not have burst yet. Thawing it slowly with warm air, not a flame, and opening the faucet lets water move once it melts. If a section split, water appears as the ice melts, so keep the main valve closed until the repair is done.

Cracked, broken and pinhole pipes: copper, galvanized and plastic repairs

Not every pipe failure is a dramatic burst. Callers describe a galvanized pipe that split without warning, a copper line with a pinhole, a leak in a wall that cannot be shut off and a joint that weeps at every fitting. Each has a different repair.

A split section is cut out and replaced with new pipe and fittings. A pinhole in copper may take a coupling, though several pinholes mean the pipe is wearing out and a repipe costs less than repeat visits. Galvanized pipe that cracks at threaded joints is usually a sign to replace the run.

Repairs in older houses can reveal more old pipe behind the fix. We show you the condition of the nearby pipe and quote the next section before the wall is closed.

Preventing the next burst: pressure and freeze protection

The International Plumbing Code limits static water pressure at fixtures to 80 pounds per square inch, and a pressure regulator is required above that. A cheap gauge screws onto a hose bib and shows your actual pressure. Readings that creep above 80 strain every fitting in the house.

For cold snaps, insulate exposed pipes in crawlspaces and attics, disconnect hoses, and let a faucet drip on the coldest nights if a line has frozen before. Know where your main shutoff is.

What to ask the plumber after the repair

A good repair comes with answers.

  • 1What caused it? Age, pressure, freezing or a bad fitting each point to a different prevention step.
  • 2Is the rest of this run sound? A failing section often sits next to others in the same condition.
  • 3Is the pressure in range? Ask for a pressure reading.
  • 4What should I tell my insurer? Get the invoice and a written cause.

Cold nights in the Midlands

Hard freezes are occasional in Richland County and Lexington County, which is why crawlspace and attic lines catch homeowners off guard. If the pipe that split sits on the street side of the meter, call Columbia Water or your own city utility first. Cleanup is separate, and a house that sits empty near Lake Murray can lose water for days before anyone notices.

The first ten minutes after a pipe lets go

Close the main valve first, then open the lowest faucet in the house to drain what is left in the lines. Switch off the water heater at its breaker or set a gas control to off, because a heater that keeps heating with an empty tank can ruin its elements. Photograph the break and the water before you move anything.

Then call. A technician who knows the pipe is galvanized, copper or PEX, and roughly where the break sits, arrives with the right couplings and shutoff tools instead of making a second trip.

What the repair itself involves

A plumber isolates the damaged run, cuts out the failed section and joins a new length with press fittings, soldered copper or push-to-connect couplings, depending on the pipe. The line is then pressure tested before the walls or floor are closed. A repair that is not tested can leak again the first time the water comes back on.

Older galvanized pipe adds one more step. Corrosion rarely stops at the break, so the technician checks the pipe on both sides and tells you if the next failure is close. If it is, repiping in one planned job usually costs less than a string of emergency calls.

Why pipes burst in the Midlands

Hard freezes are occasional here, which is why uninsulated crawlspace lines, attic runs and hose bibs catch homeowners off guard. Pressure is the other cause. The International Plumbing Code calls for a pressure-reducing valve when supply pressure is above 80 psi, and a house without one stresses every joint a little more each day.

If the break is on the street side of the meter, the utility owns it, so call Columbia Water or your own town's utility as well. Cleanup of wet drywall and flooring is a separate job, handled under water damage restoration.

What to document for an insurance claim

Most homeowners policies cover a sudden burst, while slow corrosion and neglected maintenance are treated differently. Photograph the damaged pipe, the water and the affected rooms, and keep the failed section of pipe if you can. Write down the time you found the break and what you did to stop the water.

Policies commonly expect prompt steps to limit further damage, so keep receipts for the repair, any drying equipment and temporary housing. Call your insurer early, since the adjuster may want to see the damage before cleanup. Your plumber can write down the cause of the failure on the invoice, which helps the claim.

Preventing the next one

Insulate exposed pipes in crawlspaces, attics and garages before the first freeze, and disconnect garden hoses from hose bibs in November. During a hard freeze, let a thin stream run at the faucet farthest from the main.

Know where your main valve is and test it twice a year. A pressure gauge on a hose bib takes a minute to read and tells you whether the house is running above 80 psi.

Burst pipes in lake houses and mill-village homes

Hard freezes are uncommon in the Midlands, so pipes in crawlspaces, garages and lake houses around Chapin and Lake Murray are rarely insulated. A house used part of the year can sit through a freeze with no one home.

Older houses in West Columbia's New Brookland have galvanized lines that split at the fittings when pressure surges. Close the main first, then call.
