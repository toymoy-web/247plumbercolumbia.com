What a plumber does and does not do

In the Columbia market, basement waterproofing is largely sold by foundation and crawlspace specialists who also do structural foundation repair, crawlspace encapsulation and exterior excavation. Local foundation contractors describe clay-heavy soil and humid crawlspaces as the common conditions.

That work is a different trade from plumbing. We do not do structural foundation repair, vapor barrier encapsulation or exterior waterproofing membranes. We do sump pits and pumps, interior drainage that drains to a pump, discharge lines, and finding plumbing leaks that cause the water.

Interior drainage and sump systems

An interior drain collects water at the base of the wall or along the slab edge and carries it to a sump pit, where a pump sends it outside. It works with the house's footprint and manages water that has already reached the foundation. The pump, check valve and discharge line are plumbing work, covered on our sump pump page.

A battery backup matters. Storm outages and flood conditions often occur together.

Flood-prone parts of the Midlands

In October 2015 the Gills Creek watershed east of downtown saw dam failures and a gauge in Forest Acres logged 10.64 inches of rain in four hours. Cayce's low parts near the Congaree flood at river stages the National Weather Service lists. Basements are less common in the Midlands than in northern states, and homes that have below-grade space tend to be on sloped or lake lots.

Those conditions favor pumped drainage over any claim that a coating alone will keep water out.

Gutters, grading and what to ask a drainage contractor

Before spending on a sump system, make sure roof water leaves the property. Downspouts should discharge several feet from the foundation, and the soil should slope away from the walls. These fixes are inexpensive and sometimes solve the problem by themselves.

If you do call a foundation or drainage contractor for exterior work, ask for a written scope, ask who handles permits and ask what the warranty covers. We can coordinate the plumbing side, which is usually the pump and the discharge.

Where the discharge line is allowed to go

A sump discharge should end where the water cannot flow back toward the house, such as a splash block well away from the wall or a drainage swale. Discharge into the sanitary sewer is generally not allowed, because storm water overloads treatment systems. Your city or county sets the rules, so confirm before the line is run.

Discharging onto a neighbor's property or a public sidewalk can also create problems. A plan for where the water goes is part of every good sump installation.

Crawlspace plumbing items worth checking

Even when encapsulation is handled by someone else, plumbing in the crawlspace deserves a look. Check supply lines for sweating or leaks, drain lines for sag and support, and the water heater closet seals. Insulating exposed supply pipe protects it from cold snaps, and fixing a small drip early keeps moisture from feeding mold.

What the Midlands market looks like

Foundation and crawlspace specialists dominate this service in Richland County and Lexington County, and basements are less common here than in northern states. Your Columbia Water meter is the quickest test for a plumbing leak. Gills Creek and the Congaree River define the flood side of the story, and your own meter readings show whether a plumbing leak sits behind the damp.

Crawlspaces and low ground from Cayce to Hopkins

Basements are rare in the Midlands, so most waterproofing work means crawlspaces. Low ground along the Congaree in Cayce and West Columbia and across Lower Richland near Hopkins is where water stands under floors longest after heavy rain.

Gutter discharge, grading and a crawlspace vapor barrier usually come before any pump. Our Hopkins page explains how water behaves on that side of the county.
