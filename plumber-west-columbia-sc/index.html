New Brookland and the oldest pipes in West Columbia

The heart of the city is the New Brookland Historic District around Meeting and State streets. The village grew up around the Columbia Mills textile plant, which began production in 1894, and a fire on March 3, 1905 destroyed the community. The following ten years went to rebuilding it, so the oldest pipes in the city are roughly a century old, and a camera check before a remodel costs far less than a leak behind new drywall.

That timeline tells you what is likely inside the walls. Houses rebuilt between 1905 and the 1920s were plumbed with galvanized steel supply lines and cast iron or clay drains, and many have been patched piece by piece since. Expect mixed materials.

A repair on one joint can stress its neighbor, so a technician should look at the nearby run before the wall is closed. Dispatch is open around the clock, so a burst joint at 3 a.m. in one of these houses gets the same answer as one at noon.

Flooding along the Congaree and its creeks

The city's own planning documents mark floodplain along both rivers and along Double Branch Creek and Senn Branch Creek. In October 2015 the Congaree crested at 31.8 feet, and the mayor said in 2024 that the crest had a direct impact on homes in Congaree Park and Quail Hollow Village.

If you live near the river or either creek, check three things before the next big rain. Test the sump pump and its battery backup, ask whether a backwater valve protects your sewer line, and confirm where your main water shutoff is.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC), which South Carolina adopts with state changes, calls for backwater valves on fixtures that sit below the next upstream manhole in the public sewer. Annual backflow testing also matters for lawn irrigation and commercial properties.

Before we arrive: a burst pipe or active leak in West Columbia

Water moves fast through a mill-village house with mixed old pipe. These four steps limit the damage in the minutes before a technician reaches you.

1

Shut off the main valve

Look where the line enters the house, which in older West Columbia homes is often a crawlspace or utility closet. A quarter-turn lever closes at once, while an old round gate valve may need several full turns, so test it before an emergency. If it will not close, say so on the call, because the shutoff at the meter belongs to the city.

2

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near outlets, a ceiling fixture or the panel, switch off those breakers only when the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. If the panel itself is wet, stay out and say so when you call. Never touch a switch with wet hands.

3

Clear the area

Move rugs, electronics and cardboard out of the path of the water, and lift small items off the floor. Take photographs before you clean up, since they support an insurance claim later. Keep children and pets out of any room with standing water.

Four signs a West Columbia drain or supply line is failing

These are the symptoms that most often point to age in a mill-village house.

Slow main drain

Clay and cast iron laterals under older lots collect roots and scale. A camera shows the cause before anyone digs, and sewer line repair follows from what it finds.

Rust-brown water

Galvanized supply pipe corrodes from the inside. If the color clears after a minute, ask about repiping before a pinhole leak decides the timing.

A damp spot with no visible leak

Leak detection tracks the source with listening gear and a camera instead of opening walls to guess.

Gurgling toilets after rain

Saturated ground can overload a sewer that was already marginal. Stop using water and call.

Who supplies your water here

West Columbia runs its own water system, separate from Columbia Water. When the Town of Lexington closed its water plant on October 1, 1999, it began buying water from the West Columbia plant. The city is also a member of the Joint Municipal Water and Sewer Commission, which serves unincorporated parts of Lexington County.

The practical point is hardness. Figures quoted for Columbia city water do not describe West Columbia water. Ask the city for its latest water quality report before you spend money on a softener, and compare that number with the symptoms you actually see.
