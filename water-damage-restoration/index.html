The first hour after water damage

What you do before help arrives changes the outcome.

  • 1Stop the source. Close the main valve or the supply to the failed fixture. A plumber handles the repair.
  • 2Stay out of rooms with electrical hazards. If water reaches outlets, appliances or the panel, do not touch them.
  • 3Remove what you safely can. Lift furniture, rugs and valuables off wet surfaces.
  • 4Photograph everything. Take photos and video before moving anything, for your insurer.
  • 5Call us. Tell us the source and how long the area has been wet.

Not all water is the same: the three categories

Industry guidance, the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, sorts water by contamination level. The category decides what can be saved.

Category 1 (clean)Supply line break, sink overflowDrying often saves materials if done quickly

How restoration works

The crew assesses the area, finds the extent of moisture with meters and thermal cameras, and removes standing water. Air movers and dehumidifiers then dry the structure while readings are tracked daily until they reach normal levels.

The crew removes materials that cannot dry, such as soaked insulation or sagging drywall. Cleaning and antimicrobial treatment follow where the water type calls for it. The rebuild, new drywall, paint and flooring, comes last.

Why speed matters: the 24 to 48 hour window

EPA guidance says materials that stay wet longer than 24 to 48 hours risk mold growth. South Carolina's humid summers speed that up. We make no health claims about mold, and anyone worried about health effects should talk to a physician.

If the water came from a failed pipe, the pipe still needs repair. See burst pipe repair for that side of the job, which uses a different line from this page.

Floodwater is a different situation

During the October 2015 flood, the Columbia Canal breached, a gauge on Gills Creek in Forest Acres logged 10.64 inches of rain in four hours, and dams failed. Floodwater is Category 3 water. It carries sewage and soil, and porous materials generally cannot be saved.

If your home flooded from outside, call your insurer and a flood adjuster as well, because standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood. Our sump pump page covers prevention.

Hiring a restoration contractor: what to ask

A short set of questions protects you at a stressful time.

  • 1Is there a written scope? It should list rooms, equipment and what will be removed.
  • 2Will you log moisture readings? Daily logs show the drying progress and support the claim.
  • 3How do you handle contaminated water? Category 3 losses need removal and protective gear.
  • 4What is the payment arrangement? Large up-front payments with no scope are a red flag.
  • 5What credentials can you show? Ask for proof of insurance and any certifications claimed.

Contents: what to save and what to discard

Photographs, hard furniture and non-porous items can often be cleaned and saved after clean-water losses. Most owners discard soft goods that sat in contaminated water, such as mattresses and upholstered furniture. Make a list with photos before disposal, because the insurer will want one.

Floods, boil advisories and who to call

During the October 2015 flood, the Columbia Canal breached and the city issued a boil advisory through Columbia Water. Standard homeowners policies in Richland County and Lexington County usually exclude flood, and the National Flood Insurance Program, which typically has a 30-day waiting period, covers it. The Gills Creek watershed and the Congaree floodplain are the highest-risk parts of the Midlands.

Water damage from storms and failed lines across the Midlands

Heavy rain and river rise reach different parts of the Midlands in different ways. Low streets beside the Congaree, the Gills Creek valley and the bottomland around Hopkins are the places where water stays longest.

In the Congaree Vista, ground-floor shops and restaurants in converted warehouses can take water from a failed supply line or sprinkler pipe, while plaster-wall houses in Shandon need slow, careful drying.
