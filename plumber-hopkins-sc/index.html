Lower Richland: space, farmland and long service lines

Hopkins is a census-designated place in Richland County with a 2020 population of 2,514. It sits about 11 miles southeast of downtown Columbia along the Garners Ferry Road side of the county, and Congaree National Park, the state's only national park, lies to the southeast.

Homes here sit on larger lots than in the city, and the water service line from the road can be long. A leak on a long line can run unseen under a yard for weeks before the bill or a soft patch gives it away. Leak detection and water line repair are the usual pair of calls. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, which counts most on rural roads where help is farther away.

Water and sewer service in Lower Richland is still growing

Richland County has been extending water and sewer lines through Lower Richland, so two neighbors on the same road can be on different systems. Some homes use county or city service while others still rely on private wells and septic tanks.

Before we arrive: a burst pipe or active leak in Hopkins

On a rural lot the shutoff may be at the road, at a well pressure tank or at the house. Work through these four steps.

1

Find the right shutoff

On county or city water, the main valve is where the line enters the house and the meter shutoff is at the road. On a well, close the valve after the pressure tank, and switch off the pump breaker only if the panel is dry. If you are unsure what you are looking at, describe it on the call.

2

Keep power away from water

Stay out of a crawlspace, garage or shed with standing water, even a few inches. Leave a sump pump running if its outlet is dry. Turn off breakers for wet circuits only when the panel is dry and reachable.

3

Slow the water and save what matters

Lift boxes, tools and electronics off the floor and move rugs out of the path. Take photographs of the waterline before cleanup, because a flood or leak claim relies on them. Keep children and pets out of the area.

Low ground near the Congaree floodplain

Lower Richland sits on the Congaree River side of the county, and low ground holds water after heavy rain. Water that stays near a foundation can reach crawlspaces and sump pits long before any river crests.

Test a sump pump and its backup before storm season, and keep downspouts discharging well away from the house. After a flood, a water damage crew should start drying quickly. Backwater valves fall under the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which South Carolina adopts with state changes.

What fails first in a Lower Richland house

These four symptoms point to the usual suspects.

Low pressure at every fixture

On a well, a failing pressure tank or pump switch is the usual cause. On county or city water, the regulator at the house comes first.

Hot water that runs out fast

Sediment cuts a tank heater's capacity. A flush or repair may help, while a tank past 10 to 12 years often needs replacement.

A yard that stays soggy near the house

Check the downspouts first. If they are fine, a leak on the service line or a failing drain is next.

Toilets that bubble when the shower drains

That can mean a clog in the main line, roots or a septic tank that needs attention. Drain cleaning shows which side of the house the problem is on.
