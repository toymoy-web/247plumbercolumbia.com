When a full replacement beats another patch

A spot repair fixes one failed section. Replacement makes sense when the camera shows damage along the run, a long sag that holds water, or pipe material that is breaking down. Repeating a repair every year or two costs more than one planned replacement.

Fiber conduit sold decades ago as Orangeburg pipe, cast iron that has scaled through and clay with joint after joint of root intrusion are the usual candidates. A single failed joint on an otherwise sound line is not. Replacement pipe and fittings must meet the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as South Carolina adopts it, and the inspector checks slope and bedding.

Trench or trenchless: comparing the methods

Both methods end with a new, sealed pipe. The difference is how much of your yard is disturbed.

Trench replacementTrenchless (pipe bursting or lining)
How it worksDig along the line and lay new pipePull or line a new pipe through the old path
Yard disturbanceA continuous trenchEntry and exit pits only
Fixes sagsYes, grade can be correctedBursting follows the old path, lining cannot fix a sag
Best whenThe line is shallow, sagging or the yard is openDriveways, trees or landscaping sit over the line

Before the work starts

A good plan prevents most surprises on a replacement.

  • 1Get the camera recording. It defines the scope and lets you compare quotes fairly.
  • 2Call 811. Utility locates are free and prevent line strikes.
  • 3Ask about permits. Work in the right of way or tying into the main may need approval and an inspection.
  • 4Plan the restoration. Ask who rebuilds the driveway, sod and landscaping, and in what condition.
  • 5Know the backwater valve option. A new lateral is the best time to add one on a low-lying lot.

Where the oldest lines are

West Columbia rebuilt after a fire in March 1905, and the laterals serving the old mill-village houses date from that era or later patches of it. Forest Acres houses are mostly mid-century, with long laterals on large lots.

Neither history proves a problem on your street. A camera does. It costs far less than guessing.

Replacement and the rest of the house

A failing lateral often travels with other aging parts, and some owners combine the work with repiping or a water service line replacement while the yard is open. A shared trench can save money, though two permits and two inspections still apply.

Questions to ask any sewer contractor

A short list of questions screens out a lot of risk.

  • 1Is the scope in writing? Footage, depth, method and restoration should all appear.
  • 2Who pulls the permit? The contractor normally does and passes the inspection.
  • 3What is the warranty? Ask for the term and what it covers in writing.
  • 4What happens if you hit rock or an unmarked line? A fair contract explains how surprises are handled.

Permits, locates and the county office

A new lateral that touches the right of way usually needs approval from Columbia Water, your city or the county, and Richland County and Lexington County each have their own offices, so ask which one applies. 811 marks buried lines at no charge before digging. In Cayce, where the Congaree River floods, ask the city about water table depth and backwater valves before the trench opens.

When the whole lateral has to go

Elmwood Park was listed as a National Register historic district in 1991, so ask the city whether a historic district adds any review step before a trench is dug on your street. Yard restoration and the route around mature trees matter as much as the pipe itself in neighborhoods like that.

In Hopkins and the rest of Lower Richland, longer laterals and septic connections change the route and the cost. Tell us early if you are on a septic tank, county sewer or a private line.

What changes the price of a new sewer line

Replacement is the largest sewer job, and these factors drive the total.

Length and depthLonger, deeper runs cost more.
MethodTrenchless can cost more per foot but save restoration.
Surface restorationConcrete, paving and landscaping are real costs.
Connection to the mainTap or repair work at the public sewer adds permits.

Insurance usually does not pay to replace a worn-out line. Some policies offer service line coverage, so ask yours.
