Do you need a softener? Test first

Columbia Water states that its water is 1 to 2 grains per gallon and describes it as moderately soft. Many online guides claim Columbia has hard water, but those pages are written by companies that sell softeners. For a city customer, the utility's own figure is the better number.

Cayce and West Columbia run their own water systems, and the Town of Lexington buys water from the City of West Columbia. Their hardness figures are not Columbia Water's. Ask your utility for its latest water quality report, or test the tap yourself.

Hardness bands and what to do about each

These are the commonly used classification bands. Compare your measured number with them.

Signs your existing softener needs repair

If you already own a softener, these symptoms usually point to a fixable fault.

  • 1Hard water returns. Check that the salt tank has salt and the brine is drawing. A salt bridge can look like a full tank.
  • 2Salt tank is empty too soon. A leaking valve or a stuck regeneration cycle wastes salt.
  • 3Water runs to the drain constantly. The valve is stuck between cycles and needs service.
  • 4The display is blank or wrong. A power adapter, board or clock battery is the usual cause.

Installing a softener the right way

A softener needs a bypass valve, a drain connection with an air gap, and a nearby outlet. The drain line is the part most often installed wrong, and a bad drain can back brine into the unit or onto the floor. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires that air gap so wastewater cannot siphon back, and NSF/ANSI 44 is the standard that certifies residential cation exchange softeners.

Size the unit from the household's water use and the measured hardness, not from the house's square footage. Oversizing wastes salt and water. On a well, softened water also slows scale inside a water heater, which is worth planning for.

Salt, potassium and keeping a softener healthy

Softeners regenerate with sodium chloride or potassium chloride. Potassium costs more and is useful where discharge or sodium intake is a concern. Keep the brine tank at least a quarter full, break up any salt bridge, and clean the tank once a year.

Resin beads lose capacity slowly over many years. A softener that no longer softens after a long service life usually needs resin or a new valve.

Well water is a different conversation

Private wells can carry iron, sulfur and tannins, which a basic softener handles poorly. A lab water test tells you what is there before you spend on equipment. Treatment for iron or sulfur may need a different system ahead of the softener. We can install equipment, but the test comes first.

Softener questions across two counties

Columbia Water reports 1 to 2 grains per gallon in Richland County, so most city customers do not need a softener. Private wells in rural Lexington County can differ widely, and a lab test beats guessing. Columbia Water draws from Lake Murray and the Broad River Diversion Canal, while Cayce, West Columbia and the Town of Lexington each have their own utility reports to check.

What hardness looks like in daily life

Typical symptoms include crusty faucets, spotted glasses, a stained tub, stiff laundry and a water heater that rumbles from scale. If none of those show up and your report reads under 3.5 grains per gallon, a softener has little to fix.

Softeners on well water at the edge of the county

City water in Columbia is soft, so a softener in Shandon or Five Points rarely pays for itself. Private wells in Hopkins, on Blythewood's outer lots and in parts of Lexington County are where hardness and iron can show up.

Test the water first, and tell us if you are on a well or a town system.

Where softening meets filtration

Softeners remove hardness, not chloramine, and chloramine is the main thing most Columbia customers taste. For that, read our page on whole-home water filtration. If you live in Lexington outside the town's water system, a well test is the right first move.
