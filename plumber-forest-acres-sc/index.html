Mid-century brick, ranch and split-level homes

Most Forest Acres houses are brick ranches, split-levels and other mid-century designs on lots that commonly run from half an acre to a full acre. The city incorporated in 1935 and had its own water lines by 1939, so plumbing here is 70 to 90 years old in places. Galvanized pipe commonly lasts 40 to 50 years, which is why one repair is often followed by another leak elsewhere in the same run.

Inside the walls, expect galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside, cast iron drains that crack or scale, and original water heater closets. Which pipe do you have? A five-minute check

You can learn a lot about a mid-century house with a magnet and a coin.

  • 1Magnet on the supply pipe. If it sticks to an exposed pipe near the water heater, that pipe is galvanized steel or iron. Copper is not magnetic.
  • 2Scratch test. Scrape a small spot with a coin. Copper shows a bright orange-brown, while galvanized steel shows dull gray.
  • 3Look at the drain stack. Cast iron is black, heavy and rings when tapped. PVC is white or gray and sounds hollow.
  • 4Run the hot tap for a minute. Brown water that clears is the classic sign of corroding galvanized pipe.

Before we arrive: a burst pipe or active leak in a Forest Acres house

Mid-century houses hide pipe inside slabs, plaster and closets, so the first minutes are about stopping the supply and protecting what is still dry.

1

Close the main

In most Forest Acres houses the main valve is near the water heater closet or at the front foundation wall. Turn it clockwise until it stops, then open a low faucet to drain the lines. If a warm floor or a meter that keeps turning brought you here, the leak may sit under the slab, and closing the main is still the right first move.

2

Switch off wet circuits

Turn off breakers for rooms with water near outlets, fans or ceiling lights, but only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. A leaking ceiling can fill a light fixture, so leave that switch off until it dries. If anything sparks or smells burnt, leave the house and call 911 first.

3

Protect the floors

Lift area rugs and slide furniture off the wet spot onto foil or plastic. Hardwood and plaster in an older house begin to stain within hours, so soak up standing water with towels. Take photos for your insurer before cleanup.

4

Mature trees, long laterals and the sewer line

Big lots and mature landscaping are what people like about Forest Acres. They also mean a long sewer lateral and plenty of roots looking for a joint. Roots enter clay and cast iron lines at the seams, and a snaked drain that clogs again within weeks usually has roots behind it.

Hydro jetting clears root masses and scale that a cable only punches through. If a camera shows a sagging or cracked section, sewer line replacement is the permanent answer.

What moves the price on a mid-century house

Pricing varies more in a mid-century house than in a new one, and the reasons are predictable. Access comes first. A supply line in an open crawlspace is a short job, while the same line inside a slab or behind plaster takes longer. Pipe material is next, because old galvanized fittings often snap when disturbed and turn a one-joint repair into a larger one.

After that come the length of the run, whether a permit applies, and the time of day. You hear a total before work starts, so these factors appear in the number and not as surprises later. Insurers tend to treat a sudden burst differently from slow corrosion, so ask yours which one applies before you file.

Gills Creek and heavy rain

During the October 2015 storm, one gauge on Gills Creek in Forest Acres recorded 10.64 inches of rain in four hours. Gills Creek drains much of the east side of Columbia, and homes near it feel heavy rain first. If your lot slopes toward the creek, test your sump pump before the next storm season.

A working sump pump helps below-grade rooms and crawlspaces. For wet walls or a recurring damp slab, ask about waterproofing and drainage before damage spreads to flooring and framing.
