What happens in the first 24 hours of a water emergency

A mitigation visit has a clear sequence.

  • 1Stop or confirm the source is stopped. A plumber repairs the pipe. Mitigation assumes the water has been shut off.
  • 2Extract standing water. Pumps and wet vacuums remove what can be removed.
  • 3Remove unsalvageable materials. Soaked insulation, carpet pad and ruined drywall come out.
  • 4Protect contents. Furniture is moved or blocked up and valuables are packed away from wet areas.
  • 5Begin drying. Air movers and dehumidifiers start, with moisture readings logged.

Documentation insurers look for

Insurers want to see the cause, the extent and the steps taken. Photos before and after, moisture readings, equipment logs and receipts all matter. A tidy record supports the claim and speeds payment.

Do not throw out ruined belongings before they have been photographed and listed. Keep the plumber's invoice as proof of the failure, and mention it to your adjuster. Our burst pipe repair page covers the repair side.

What not to do while you wait

Do not use a household vacuum on standing water. Do not turn on a ceiling fixture below a wet ceiling. Do not walk on a sagging ceiling or wet drywall. Do not run a space heater near wet areas.

If the water is sewage or floodwater, keep children and pets away and avoid contact. Those losses are Category 3 and need extra precautions.

Why speed matters in the Midlands

South Carolina's warm, humid climate speeds mold growth, and EPA guidance says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. In October 2015 the Columbia area saw record rain, dam failures and a canal breach, and many families waited days for help. A quick start narrows the loss.

A leak from upstairs: ceilings and light fixtures

Water that comes through a ceiling can pool in the insulation above and soak the drywall. A bulging ceiling can release a lot of water at once and may collapse, so stay out from underneath and do not poke it yourself. Turn off power to that circuit from a dry location, keep the light fixture off until it has been checked, and call.

Reporting a loss in the Midlands

Call your insurer early, whether you live in Richland County or Lexington County. If the water came from the street, Columbia Water or your city utility should hear about it. Homes near the Congaree River and Gills Creek were among the most exposed in October 2015, and policies commonly expect prompt steps to limit further damage.

Stopping the spread in older and newer buildings

Mitigation starts at the source: closing the main, extracting standing water and removing wet material quickly. In plaster-wall houses in Shandon and Elmwood Park the drying plan differs from the plan for drywall houses in Blythewood and Northeast Columbia.

Moisture readings guide how long equipment runs and when a wall is dry. Ask for those readings in writing.
