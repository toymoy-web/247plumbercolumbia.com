When the Congaree rises: Cayce's flood pattern

Granby, the settlement that stood where Cayce is today, lost people in the early 1800s to flooding and disease, and the county moved its courthouse to Lexington in 1820. Flooding still shapes the city.

The National Weather Service impact list for the Congaree shows Old State Road below Cayce going under at 16 feet and the low parts of the Riverland Park subdivision flooding from about 28 feet. If your street is near the river, treat a flood warning as a plumbing task as well as a weather one.

Cayce once annexed a large stretch of floodplain and later dropped plans to build on it after deciding it was unsuitable. For homeowners near the river, the first place trouble shows is the crawlspace or sump pit. A sump pump with a battery backup and a backwater valve on the sewer line cost far less than drying out a floor. Emergency calls are dispatched around the clock, which counts most on the nights the Congaree is rising.

Before the river rises: five checks

Do these in dry weather and the next flood warning becomes a routine afternoon instead of an emergency.

  • 1Find the main shutoff. Label it and make sure everyone in the house can turn it by hand.
  • 2Lift the sump pump lid. Pour in a bucket of water and watch it start and empty the pit.
  • 3Check the backup power. A battery backup only helps if the battery is under five years old.
  • 4Raise what you can. Move the water heater controls and stored items off the floor of the crawlspace or garage.
  • 5Photograph the baseline. Before-and-after photos make an insurance claim much easier.

Before we arrive: burst pipe, active leak or water in a Cayce crawlspace

In Cayce the water is as likely to come up from the ground as out of a pipe. Start with these four steps either way.

1

Stop the supply

Close the main valve where the line enters the house, usually near the water heater or at the crawlspace access. Cayce runs its own water system, so a leak on the street side of the meter is a call to City Hall as well as to us. If the water is rising from the ground, a closed main will not stop it, and the next steps matter more.

2

Keep power away from water

Stay out of a crawlspace or garage with standing water, even a few inches. Switch off breakers for wet circuits only if the panel is dry and reachable without wading. If a sump pump is running and its outlet is dry, leave it on and tell us when you call.

3

Move what you can

Raise boxes, tools and stored items off the garage or crawlspace floor, and move rugs and electronics out of the path. Photograph the waterline before cleanup, because a flood claim leans on those pictures. If floor drains are gurgling, say so, since that points to the sewer and not the supply.

Cold snaps and hose bibs

A few nights below freezing each winter are enough to split exposed hose bib lines and uninsulated crawlspace supply pipes. Disconnect hoses in November, wrap exposed pipe, and know that a frozen line that cracked will not show it until it thaws. If water appears after a cold night, shut off the main and call for burst pipe repair before the damage spreads.

Postwar Cayce: what 1950s housing needs

The Blossom Street Bridge opened in the early 1950s and shortened the commute to Columbia, which drove growth in the decades that followed. Houses from that period commonly have galvanized or early copper supply lines, cast iron drains, and a water heater closet built for a smaller tank.

At seventy-plus years, the question is usually timing. Spot repairs on galvanized pipe tend to be followed by another failure within a year or two, so a house with repeated leaks is a candidate for repiping in one planned job. South Carolina's plumbing code, the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with state modifications, allows copper, CPVC and PEX for new supply lines.

Cayce runs its own water, so check your own numbers

Cayce operates its own water system, with a raw water pump station on the Congaree River, and its own regional wastewater plant, which opened in October 2012. That means Columbia Water's published figures do not describe Cayce tap water. Ask City Hall at 1800 12th Street for the latest water quality report.
