Snaked it, got stuck, clogged again: what this usually means

People rarely ask for jetting by name. They describe the cycle. These descriptions are the usual match.

The clog returns within weeks

A cable opens a hole in the blockage. Grease or scale on the pipe wall rebuilds the clog.

The cable stopped short

Roots, a collapsed section or a heavy buildup can stop a cable. A camera shows which.

Only the main line is slow

Buildup in a long lateral often responds to jetting when the branch lines are fine.

A restaurant or shop floor drain backs up

Commercial lines carry grease and food solids that cabling does not remove.

How a jetting visit works

The technician runs a camera through the line first to find the clog and check the pipe. A hose with a nozzle then feeds in under high pressure, pulling itself forward and scouring the wall as it retracts. A second camera pass confirms the line is clear.

Jetting is not a repair. It cleans. If the camera shows a crack or a sag, you need a repair, and our page on sewer line repair covers that.

Some utilities and contractors grade camera findings with the NASSCO Pipeline Assessment Certification Program (PACP) coding system, so ask whether your report uses it. Access is also why the International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for cleanouts on building drains, and a line without one may need a toilet pulled to reach it.

When jetting is the wrong tool

High-pressure water can worsen a pipe that is already failing. Cracked clay, broken cast iron and fiber conduit sold decades ago as Orangeburg pipe can collapse under pressure.

That is why the camera comes first. In older blocks such as the New Brookland area of West Columbia, a camera pass is cheap insurance against making a pipe problem worse.

Commercial and restaurant lines

Grease is the leading problem for food businesses. Jetting a line without maintaining the grease interceptor only moves the problem downstream, so the interceptor and the line should be serviced together.

Ask your utility about its fats, oils and grease rules before you schedule. Our page on commercial plumbing covers interceptors and floor drains.

What to expect on jetting day

A jetting visit is straightforward, but a little preparation helps.

  • 1Clear access to the cleanout. The technician needs room for a hose reel and a camera.
  • 2Protect nearby surfaces. Jetting from an indoor cleanout can splash. Ask whether drop cloths are included.
  • 3Expect a camera pass. You can watch the recording and ask about any damage it shows.
  • 4Keep water use down. Avoid flushing or running water while the hose is in the line.

Jetting before you buy or sell a house

A camera-and-jet visit during a home sale tells a buyer or seller what is in the lateral. A clean, recorded line is a selling point, and a damaged one lets both sides negotiate with facts. If a home inspection flagged slow drains, jetting plus a camera is the usual follow-up.

Emergency hydro jetting: when cabling failed and the main is backing up

Jetting becomes an emergency tool when a cable opened the line once and the backup returns within days. Repeat backups mean grease, scale or roots are coating the pipe wall, and a cable punches a hole through the middle without cleaning the sides.

If sewage is entering the house, stop water use and call. We run a camera first to confirm the pipe can take the pressure, then jet the line to scrub the walls. A line that is cracked or collapsed needs repair instead, and the camera tells us which case we have.

Restaurants and shops often need this at night, so the emergency line is also the right call for a commercial backup.

Where jetting fits in the Midlands

Restaurants and older buildings, such as those around Five Points and the Congaree Vista in Richland County, are the usual jetting customers. Ask Columbia Water about its rules on fats, oils and grease, and remember that the South Carolina Department of Environmental Services regulates wastewater, so clean the line and service the interceptor together.

What the pressure really is

Residential jetters commonly work in the thousands of pounds per square inch, with a hose tipped by nozzles that fire forward to cut and backward to pull the hose along. That force clears grease and roots, and it is the reason the camera check comes first on old clay or cast iron.

Jetting older mains in Elmwood Park and Earlewood

Elmwood Park holds shotgun houses, Queen Anne cottages and brick bungalows from the early 1900s, and Earlewood dates to 1910. Their sewer laterals are old enough that scale and roots are the usual cause of repeat clogs, and jetting scours both from the pipe wall.

Jetting is not for a broken pipe, because high pressure can make a crack worse. A camera look comes first, and jetting follows only when the pipe can take it.
