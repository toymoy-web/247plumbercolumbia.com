Two Notch Road, Clemson Road and the suburbs between them

Northeast Columbia is not a separate city. It is the suburban belt north and east of Forest Acres, centered on ZIP code 29223 and reaching toward Dentsville and Lake Carolina. Two Notch Road and Clemson Road are the main routes, and Sesquicentennial State Park at 9564 Two Notch Road is its best-known green space.

The area grew in waves, so one street can hold a 1980s ranch next to a 2000s two-story. Each decade used different pipe, different heaters and different drain slopes, which is why a first visit starts by working out which era the house belongs to. Dispatch is available around the clock for the suburban streets off Two Notch Road and Clemson Road.

Gray plastic pipe and the 1980s and 1990s question

Many houses built from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s may have polybutylene supply pipe, a gray plastic that can fail at its fittings. Look where the line enters the house and at the water heater connections. Gray plastic pipe with no brand stamp deserves a professional look.

When a leak does turn up, a single repair rarely ends the story because the nearby fittings share the same age. A planned repipe often costs less than repeated ceiling and wall repairs, and leak detection finds the failure before anyone opens drywall.

Before we arrive: a burst pipe or active leak in Northeast Columbia

In a slab house the water may be coming from under the floor, not from a visible pipe. These four steps help either way.

1

Close the main valve

Look at the front or side of the house near the hose bib, in the garage or at the water heater. If the meter box at the street is the only shutoff you can find, say so on the call. A leak that keeps running after the house valve is closed points to the service line outside.

2

Stay clear of wet electrics

Water near outlets, a ceiling fixture or the panel is a hazard. Turn off breakers for wet circuits only when the panel is dry and reachable. If you hear running water but see none, do not open walls or lift flooring yourself.

3

Protect the floors and belongings

Lift small furniture and electronics off wet flooring and move rugs to a dry room. Take photographs of the wet area and of any warm spot on the floor, since both help with an insurance claim. A warm patch on slab tile can mean a hot line is leaking below.

Slab foundations and the leaks beneath them

Many homes in this part of the Midlands are built on concrete slabs, so supply lines can run beneath the floor. A warm spot on tile, the sound of running water with every fixture off or a sudden jump in the water bill can all point to a slab leak.

A pressure test and acoustic equipment locate the leak before any concrete is cut. Slab leak repair then chooses between a spot repair, a reroute through the attic and a full repipe, depending on the age of the pipe. Any new piping follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as South Carolina adopts it.

Utilities here vary by street. Richland County's utility list names the City of Columbia, Richland County Utilities and East Richland County Public Service District among its providers, and an address with a Columbia mailing name can be served by any of them.

Calls that fit suburban subdivisions

These four account for most of the visits we make off Two Notch Road and Clemson Road.

A leaking water heater

A tank past its tenth year that weeps at the base is finished. Replacement beats repeated repair.

A clogged kitchen drain

Grease and disposal waste build up in the branch line. Disposal repair and drain cleaning fix the usual causes.

A toilet that runs or will not flush

Worn flappers and fill valves are common after a few years, and toilet repair is usually quick.

A sewage smell or gurgling drain

That points to the main line or a vent, and a sewer camera inspection shows which.
