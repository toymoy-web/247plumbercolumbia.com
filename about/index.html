What we do

Columbia 24/7 Plumbing is a call-in plumbing service for Columbia, South Carolina and the surrounding Midlands, including Forest Acres, Cayce, West Columbia, Irmo, Lexington, Blythewood, Northeast Columbia, Chapin and Hopkins. We take calls around the clock for emergencies like burst pipes, sewage backups and failed water heaters, and for planned work like repiping and new fixtures.

Our services page lists everything we cover, and our service areas page explains how the towns differ.

How a call works

The process is the same for an emergency and a planned job.

  • 1You call (844) 981-1691. Someone answers and asks what is happening.
  • 2We connect you with a plumbing professional. Work is done by an independent local professional, not by this website.
  • 3You hear the price first. The professional quotes before work begins, and you decide whether to go ahead.
  • 4The professional does the work. That professional is responsible for the license, insurance and workmanship.

What we do not do

We are honest about the limits. We do not do structural foundation repair, crawlspace encapsulation or well pump repair, and we say so on the pages where those questions come up. We do not offer legal, insurance or medical advice, and our guides say clearly when something is general information.

We also do not publish customer reviews, star ratings, named quotes or years-in-business figures on this site, because we have no verified numbers to show and will not invent them.

How we write the guides on this site

Local facts on this site come from public sources: Columbia Water's own pages and water quality reports, the South Carolina Department of Environmental Services, the National Weather Service, the state building codes council and local histories. When a number comes from a source, we try to use it as written, and when we cannot confirm something, we say less.

Plumbing practice is described the way the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the International Fuel Gas Code (IFGC) frame it, as South Carolina adopts them. Codes change, so check with your local building department for current rules.

What to expect on a first call

Describe what you see, where you are and whether water is still running. If it is, you will be asked to close the main valve first, since that stops the damage while the rest is arranged. You will be asked about the age and type of the equipment, and you will hear a price before work starts. Non-emergency estimates are free.

If the problem involves gas, we ask you to leave the building and call 911 and your gas provider first. Dominion Energy South Carolina takes gas leak calls at 800-815-0083. A plumbing appointment never comes before safety.

Why this site talks about cost factors and not price lists

Plumbing prices depend on access, materials, fuel type, permits and the time of day, so a single number on a web page would mislead more often than it would help. Each service page lists the factors that move the price, along with an honest note about what insurance usually does and does not cover. The real number comes from the professional who sees your job, before work begins.

We would rather tell you what drives the cost than publish a figure that is wrong for your house.

Why the local detail matters

Plumbing in the Midlands is not the same as plumbing in other markets. Columbia Water reports soft water and disinfects with chloramine. Cayce and West Columbia run their own utilities. The Gills Creek watershed and the Congaree floodplain flooded badly in October 2015, and housing ranges from mill-village blocks rebuilt after a 1905 fire to subdivisions built in the last few years.

That is why our town pages differ from one another and why our guides cite local sources. Generic advice often fails on specifics such as water hardness, flood exposure or pipe age.

Safety comes before sales

Some situations are not plumbing jobs yet. A gas smell, sewage in living space, water near electrical equipment and a flooded home during a storm all call for protecting people first. Our pages say so plainly, and they point to 911 and to the right utility where that applies.

We also avoid scare tactics. If a repair can wait, or a problem is cheaper to fix yourself, we say so.

Licensing and credentials

Plumbing work in South Carolina is regulated through the state's Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation. We have not listed license numbers on this site, because we will not publish credentials we cannot show. Ask any plumber for a license number and verify it with the state before work begins. A good professional will give it without hesitation.
